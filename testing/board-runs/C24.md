# Board run: C24 tick-path overhead

Status: run on CM5 2026-11-10 (probe off and on, and the window overhead, taken with `cpu-timings`).

## Check

- The tick-path overhead of a 16-stage chain with `TickProbe` off and on, against `dev`'s tick overhead numbers.
- With the probe off the run path adds one `None` check per step, so the overhead with it off must match C1's per-stage figures.
- Record the per-tick `overhead_ns` from `TickOverheadWindow::report` next to the probe-off time per tick.

## Result


Run on 2026-11-10 on the CM5 (raze2): OS image 2026.2.0-dev.993+e1097ec7, kernel 7.2.9-v8-16k, aarch64. Tested commit: `next` at bdbfe947.

- `core/host` probe tests built with `cargo test --release --no-run --target aarch64-unknown-linux-gnu -p daedalus-host` and run on the board as `probe --nocapture --test-threads=1`: 4 of 4 pass. That includes `a_warm_tick_with_a_probe_attached_allocates_nothing`, `take_tick_resets_the_probe_and_peek_does_not`, `the_window_keeps_the_last_ticks_and_derives_the_remainder` and `diagnostics_round_trip_as_json`.
- Not run: the 16-stage chain's tick time with `TickProbe` off and on, and the per-tick `overhead_ns` from `TickOverheadWindow::report`. This branch has no timing harness that drives the probe, and writing one is new code, so it was not written.

## Probe cost, before and after (probe-cost, 2026-11-11)

The probe read the clock twice a step, so a 16-stage tick made 34 reads. It now reads once at the
start of the tick, once after each step that runs, and once at the end: 18 reads. A step's time runs
from the previous reading to its own end, so the runner's work between steps is counted in the next
step's body time. `overhead_ns` is therefore only the time before the first step and after the last,
and its figure before and after are not comparable.

Same board, same binary family, three runs each, medians of 101 batches of 1,000 calls:

| Row | before, run 1 / 2 / 3 | after, run 1 / 2 / 3 | median before | median after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One clock read (`clock_read`), ns | 45.1 / 45.1 / 46.7 | 45.9 / 45.9 / 45.9 | 45.1 | 45.9 |
| Chain, probe off, ns | 1,968.4 / 1,955.2 / 1,915.2 | 1,940.1 / 1,925.7 / 2,050.0 | 1,955.2 | 1,940.1 |
| Chain, probe on, ns | 3,550.0 / 3,573.1 / 3,557.7 | 2,881.8 / 2,889.2 / 2,883.4 | 3,557.7 | 2,883.4 |
| Probe's own cost, probe on minus off, ns | 1,594.8 / 1,617.9 / 1,642.5 | 941.7 / 963.5 / 833.4 | 1,617.9 | 941.7 |
| Window `overhead_ns`, ns (meaning changed, see above) | 2,259.3 / 2,294.6 / 2,272.8 | 112.8 / 113.3 / 112.5 | 2,272.8 | 112.8 |

The probe-on cost fell by about 42%. What is left is 18 clock reads at about 46 ns each, which is
about 830 ns a tick, and the atomic updates. Sampling (timing every Kth tick) would remove more.
It needs a flag saying which ticks were timed, so that the window never mixes timed and untimed
ticks. That is not in this change.

The probe-off path is unchanged: the budget rows (`idle_tick_16` 152 instructions, `fused_chain_16_tick`
8,778 instructions, `alloc_fused_tick` 0) are inside their limits.

Dev's per-stage tick overhead on the CM5, from `docs/benchmarks-log.md` (its "Fusion" section, the
CM5 measurement at `bcc9f33`): `graph_overhead` p50 fit 1,194 ns plus 655 ns per stage unfused, and

## Reference rerun, the unfused per-stage cost (reference-rerun, 2026-11-11)

What the reference's 655 ns per stage measures: `docs/benchmarks-log.md`'s fit of `graph_overhead` p50
over the detector mirror (`crates/frame-bench/examples/detector.rs`, flat shape). That is a frame
pushed through the engine (push, tick, take of a `Payload`), and the stages carry real frame work.
It is not a per-stage cost of an empty stage. So the 117 ns in this record (our no-op chain) and
the 655 ns are different things, and the record now gives the like-for-like pair below.

Both sides on the CM5, three runs each, today:

| Row | reference (dev, `detector` flat, `DAEDALUS_NODE_FUSION` on/off), p50 per frame, ns | ours (`detector_fused`/`detector_unfused`, median of 101 batches of 1,000), ns |
|---|---|---|
| fused | 8,796 / 8,945 / 8,870, median 8,870 | 1,293.8 / 1,296.6 / 1,296.7, median 1,296.6 |
| unfused | 9,037 / 8,982 / 9,019, median 9,019 | 1,312.6 / 1,313.6 / 1,324.5, median 1,313.6 |

Result: the reference's frame is about 6.8 times ours on the same five-stage graph. The gap is the host
path: the reference pushes a payload through the engine and takes one out, ours feeds and takes raw
values. The per-stage split of that gap is not measured here. The reference's unfused frame is 1.7%
above its fused frame; ours is 1.3% above, so the fusion benefit is about the same on both sides. The
reference's `graph_overhead` slope of 655 ns per stage is therefore not a figure this run can confirm or
miss: the like-for-like number is the frame, not the stage.

## Probe every-tick cost, after the clock and bookkeeping change (probe-tick-cost, 2026-11-11)

Where the 60 ns a stage went, measured on the CM5 with a standalone aarch64 microbenchmark (median of
101 batches of 1,000 operations, three runs stable to within 0.5 ns):

| Operation | ns |
|---|---|
| `Instant::now()` based clock (the old probe clock) | 45 to 46 |
| `mrs cntvct_el0` alone (the counter; `cntfrq_el0` is 54 MHz here) | 13 to 15 |
| counter read plus the multiply to nanoseconds | 13 |
| relaxed `fetch_add` on an `AtomicU64` (no LSE on this target) | 5.9 |
| relaxed load and store | 4.6 |
| the old per-step bookkeeping without the clock (two `fetch_add`s, one load and one store) | 11.7 |
| the same with the two `fetch_add`s removed (one load and one store) | 2.3 |

So a stage paid about 45 ns of clock and about 12 ns of bookkeeping, which is the 60 ns. Two changes
follow from that:

- The probe takes its times from a `ProbeClock`. On aarch64 Linux `best_clock` returns the counter
  register, scaled by a multiplier computed once from `cntfrq_el0` at load. Other platforms keep the
  host's clock function. Only differences of readings are used, so the counter's starting point does
  not matter. The counter read is in `core/runner` with a SAFETY comment, the only crate on the
  boundary list that reads it.
- A serial tick (the runner's own tick, one step after another) takes no per-step body sum: its body
  time is the last reading less the first, which is the sum of its steps' times exactly. The step count
  is added once per tick from the tick report, timed or not. A parallel tick keeps its per-step body
  sum, since its steps overlap and the readings cannot be telescoped.

Same board, same session, three runs each, `daedalus-cpu-timings`, medians of 101 batches of 1,000
calls, the 16-stage chain with one feed and one take a tick. "Before" is `origin/next` at ef131899, built in its own worktree; "after" is this change.

| Row | before, run 1 / 2 / 3 | after, run 1 / 2 / 3 | median before | median after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tick, probe off, ns | 1,951.8 / 1,968.1 / 1,968.2 | 1,939.1 / 1,926.7 / 1,939.2 | 1,968.1 | 1,939.1 |
| Tick, every tick (K=1), ns | 2,928.7 / 2,924.4 / 2,916.8 | 2,025.3 / 2,009.5 / 2,017.3 | 2,924.4 | 2,017.3 |
| Tick, every 8th (K=8), ns | 2,121.9 / 2,108.7 / 2,109.6 | 1,976.5 / 1,975.8 / 1,969.7 | 2,109.6 | 1,975.8 |
| Tick, every 64th (K=64), ns | 2,049.2 / 1,998.6 / 1,998.7 | 1,945.4 / 1,948.7 / 1,973.2 | 1,998.7 | 1,948.7 |
| Window mean `overhead_ns` at K=1, ns | 110.3 / 110.5 / 109.9 | 67.6 / 67.8 / 67.4 | 110.3 | 67.6 |

The probe's cost at every tick fell from about 956 ns to about 78 ns a tick over the probe-off tick,
which is about 5 ns a stage. Probe off is unchanged within noise.

Budget rows, the probe-off path (`idle_tick_16`, `fused_chain_16_tick`, `plugin_node_tick`, and the
allocation rows): all inside their limits. `idle_tick_16` 160 Ir and `fused_chain_16_tick` 8,954 Ir
here, against 158 and 8,949 Ir for the untouched `origin/next` tree on the same run, so the change adds
2 and 5 Ir to the probe-off path. The `alloc_*` rows are 0.

Not done: x86_64 TSC. It is invariant on most parts, but the invariance bit and user-space readability
were not checked on a machine that exercises them, so x86_64 keeps the host clock. A chip build keeps
the host's clock too.

Not measured: the parallel tick's timings. Its body sum still uses per-step atomics and a shared mark,
as before this change; it is not exact across lanes.

## Probe every-tick cost on x86_64 Linux, the TSC (probe-tick-cost, 2026-11-11)

Same change as the aarch64 counter above, on x86_64: `best_clock` takes the time stamp counter when
the CPU's counter is invariant (CPUID 0x80000007 EDX bit 8) and the kernel's clocksource is `tsc`.
The frequency comes from CPUID 0x15, then 0x16, and otherwise from one 10 ms calibration against the
host's monotonic clock; a result outside 0.5 to 10 GHz keeps the host clock. This desktop is an AMD
Ryzen 9 5900XT. Its CPUID leaves 0x15 and 0x16 are absent, so the frequency came from calibration. The
clocksource is `tsc`, and the counter is invariant, so the TSC path was taken. `cpu-timings` prints the
clock it chose (`probe clock: tsc`).

The desktop was not quiet: its load average was 61 during the runs, from other processes, so the
figures spread widely. Seven interleaved rounds per row, `taskset -c 8`, medians of 101 batches of
1,000 calls per run, with the min and max of the seven medians:

| Row | before, median (min to max), ns | after, median (min to max), ns |
|---|---|---|
| Tick, probe off | 1,674.3 (1,603.3 to 1,756.4) | 1,710.0 (1,117.8 to 1,784.2) |
| Tick, every tick (K=1) | 2,385.6 (1,973.3 to 2,439.8) | 1,852.9 (1,651.1 to 2,012.7) |
| Tick, every 8th (K=8) | 1,786.1 (1,182.0 to 1,951.9) | 1,766.2 (1,672.7 to 1,835.7) |
| Tick, every 64th (K=64) | 1,644.3 (1,159.4 to 1,775.4) | 1,772.8 (1,651.3 to 1,828.3) |

"Before" is the tree at 91dddb72, where x86_64 used the host's monotonic clock. "After" is this
change. The every-tick row shows the saving: about 530 ns a tick less, which is about 33 ns a step
over the 16 stages. The sampled rows, and the probe-off row, are inside the spread: at this load the
desktop cannot resolve a difference of 50 to 100 ns at K=8 and K=64, so those rows are not evidence of
a change either way. A quiet machine would be needed for that.

Budget rows on this desktop, probe-off path: `idle_tick_16` 160 Ir, `fused_chain_16_tick` 8,950 Ir,
`plugin_node_tick` 3,225 Ir, and the `alloc_*` rows 0, all inside their limits.
