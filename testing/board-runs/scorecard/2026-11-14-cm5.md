What the figures say:

- A worker hand-over is a copy. A worker is its own process and a value crosses its stdin and
  stdout pipes: about eight passes over each megabyte, and the fixture's own reversal of it,
  through 64 KiB pipe chunks. Large values moving as shared memory instead is the next piece of
  work.
- The daemon's RSS rises while its trace of the last 1024 requests fills, about 150 kB at its
  cap, and then stays flat: on the PC, 5.0 MB at 1000 round trips and 2.6 MB at 3000 and 6000.
  The socket path allocates per request (decoding, and the trace entry's two strings); the
  in-process host does not.
- The first daemon run found a defect: a daemon whose socket file was removed never stopped.
  #263 fixed it, and the rerun's daemon stopped on SIGTERM.
