## Build

- Several processes share one GPU. A host that has to take turns gives the backend a hook: asked before the device is given work, told when the work is done. With no hook it costs nothing.
- The backend knows nothing about who else wants the GPU or how the turn is decided, and depends on nothing that does. The host fills the hook.

## Proof

`backends/wgpu/src/lease.rs` unit tests: no hook counts nothing, overlapping work is one acquire and one release, and changing the hook with work out keeps each hook's calls paired. `backends/wgpu/tests/lease.rs`, on a device: a lease set before the device opens is asked for each round of shader work and given back after it, taking it away stops the calls, and setting it again starts them. `examples/04_gpu/tests/dmabuf.rs`: a graph run asks the lease and has given it back when the graph is done.

## Status

Done.

- `GpuLease` has `acquire` and `release`. `WgpuBackend::set_lease(Option<Arc<dyn GpuLease>>)` sets it on the open device and on any the backend opens later, and opens nothing itself.
- The count is per device: the first submission with none in flight acquires, and the last to finish releases. Every submit the backend makes goes through it: the eight queue submits (region, shader, fragment pass, readback, texture read, buffer clear and read, flushed writes) and the chain recorded with Vulkan itself.
- A queue submit gives its part back from the device's own "submitted work done" callback. A chain has no callback, so its part is kept with the chain's place in the fence table and given back when its fence is polled ready or failed, when the chain runs again, or when the region is dropped.
- With no lease set, a submit loads one flag. Nothing is locked or allocated.
- The lease is on the device, not the backend: backends that share the process's device share one lease, and the last one set is the one asked.
